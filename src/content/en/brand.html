01 · Logo

The Facet mark and wordmark

A cube seen in isometric view: three facets (teal top, blue right, navy left) around an amber core, beside a geometric wordmark and the descriptor “Data & AI Governance”. Every graphic on the site is built from this cube.

What the mark says

  • The cube: a stable, governed structure, built piece by piece.
  • Three facets: data, AI and governance as one connected whole.
  • Amber core: human judgment at the center, the one decision point.

Clear space

Keep clear space around the logo of at least twice the height of the amber core. Never place it on a pattern without a solid panel.

Minimum size

Full logo: 160px wide on screen (35mm in print). Mark on its own: 16px. Below 160px, use the mark.

Don’t

Recolor, stretch, rotate, outline or add effects. Don’t separate the descriptor from the wordmark or rebuild the logo in live text.

Always link to the master files in /assets/ (logo.svg, logo-reverse.svg, mark.svg, logo-mark-reverse.svg) rather than copying artwork.

02 · Color

Frost paper, navy ink, three facets and one amber core

Every page, public and portal, sits on cool frost paper with white sheets. Navy is the frame and teal is action. Amber is reserved for membership actions (Join, Apply, Become a member): the header Join button plus at most one amber button on the page. The amber core of the cube in the logo and the illustrations is identity, not a control.

04 · Graphics

The Facet graphic system

No stock photography. Every image is generated geometry on the logo’s 30° axes: a hairline lattice, cube assemblies in the three face colors with one amber core, a 30° facet cut and hex-framed monograms.

Isometric lattice

Hairline grid at 30°, 90° and 150° (52×30 tile), navy at 7.5% on light, white at 7% on navy. Always masked to fade, and only behind empty space.

Facet cut

A 30° chamfer on one corner of filled panels: the CTA panel, the membership card. At most one per view.

CSSKJM

Hex monograms

Initials on the cube’s silhouette, in navy, teal or amber faces. Used for people everywhere; never a generic silhouette.

Cube assemblies

Flat faces only (no gradients, no shadows on art), the three face colors and their tints, and one amber core per view. Files live in /assets/illustrations/; in React use <FacetArt name="…" /> from src/components/ui/Facet.tsx, which can also assemble the pieces once on load.

05 · Icons

One line icon set

24px grid, 1.5px stroke, round caps, currentColor. Teal 700 on light, Teal 300 on navy. In React: <Icon name="calendar" /> from src/components/ui/Icon.tsx.

06 · Motion

Assemble, then stay still

Motion is quiet and CSS-only. Pieces slide 12px along the isometric axis as they appear, hover lifts cards by 2px, and nothing loops or autoplays. With reduced motion, everything is visible and static.

  • Durations

    120ms (color), 200ms (hover), 320ms (panels), 520ms (entrances). One easing: cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1).

  • Never gate content

    Content is visible without animation. Scroll reveals use animation-timeline: view() only where supported.

  • Reduced motion

    All animation lives inside prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference; a global rule removes the rest.

07 · Voice & tone

Voice & tone

We write like a trusted professional body: clear, evidence-based and honest about what exists today.

  • Authoritative, not academic. Plain language; define jargon.
  • Practical. Lead with what the reader can do.
  • Even-handed. No hype about AI, and no fear either.
  • Honest about status. Label anything not yet live as Planned, Proposed or Coming soon. No invented numbers, dates or partners.
  • Consistent vocabulary. Uncertainty, anticipation, governance, responsible scaling, resilience.
  • Inclusive and global. Bilingual (EN/FR) and globally relevant.
  • Transparent. Disclose AI use, sponsors and affiliations.
Do

“Practical guidance to help your organization anticipate emerging AI risks and scale responsibly.”

Don’t

“Unlock revolutionary AI-powered governance synergies!”

Do

“Enterprise foresight working group (proposed). Register your interest.”

Don’t

“Join thousands of leaders in our working group”, when it doesn’t exist yet.
